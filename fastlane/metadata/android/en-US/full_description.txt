The app is in French: it is made for French-speaking people, and its content comes from violentometres.fr.

Is this normal? Is this serious? A "violentomètre" (violence meter) lays out everyday situations from green (all is well) to red (danger), to help recognise violence, name it, and know what to do.

The app takes its content from violentometres.fr, and works offline.

FOUR ENTRY POINTS

• Find help: helplines and emergency numbers, one tap away. 3919, 17, 114 by text, 119, 3018, and more.
• Take stock: a few questions, one at a time, about what you are going through, then a result and next steps. About your situation in general, a specific scale, or a whole learning path.
• Testimonies: read what others have been through, and share your own, with no account and no name.
• Understand: learning paths, one card per step.

Dozens of scales, in relationships, the family, at work, at school, online, in healthcare, with institutions, in the street, and elsewhere. And you can build your own: pick situations from a library of more than a thousand, rewrite them in your own words, and rank them yourself.

DISCREET

• On the home screen, it can appear as Calculator, and work as a real one. Your code, then =, opens it.
• Your answers never leave the phone. By default nothing is kept: everything is gone when you leave the app.
• "Quitter vite" (leave quickly), at the top of every screen, erases everything, closes the app and removes it from recent apps.
• No account, no ads, no trackers, no notifications.

The app also says what it does not protect against: someone searching the phone settings or your Google account, someone forcing you to open it, spyware. And the name of this listing stays visible in the store history.

A TOOL, NOT A DIAGNOSIS

The result of taking stock is not professional advice. It helps put words on things, and know who to turn to. In danger, call 17, or text 114.
